# Decisions

## Why this approach?

I chose RunwayDesk, a launch readiness workspace for small SaaS teams, because it naturally supports a concrete product interface in the hero and showcase. The obvious alternative was a generic AI productivity homepage, but that would risk vague copy, decorative visuals, and fake-sounding claims. A release operations product gives the page specific user intent, believable workflows, and useful dashboard details without needing fabricated social proof.

## Trade-off

Under the time limit, I built a polished static front-end demo instead of a fully persisted product prototype. The interaction changes the launch workspace lens and shows product thinking, but it does not save data or model a complete release workflow. With a full week, I would add editable tasks, richer keyboard navigation, more empty/error states, and visual QA across additional devices and browsers.

## AI usage

AI assisted with product concept selection, copy direction, component structure, and implementation. The final work was personally reviewed and changed for the assignment constraints: no fake testimonials, no fake logos, no fake metrics, Part 2 only, responsive behavior, accessibility basics, production build readiness, and a focused product showcase.
